Alternative Solutions Guide for Small System Water Reuse and
Stormwater Use

The growing need for water conservation and more efficient use of water resources in Alberta has resulted
in the development of guidance for small water reuse and stormwater use systems (serving a single
property). Water reuse consists of either wastewater or stormwater, which has been treated to a quality
suitable for a specific end-use. These systems may require an approved alternative solution as defined by
the National Plumbing Code as referenced in the Plumbing Code Regulation.

This notice is intended to provide information to guide applicants toward ensuring all necessary details are
prepared and regulatory requirements, including the applicable authorities, have been considered when
proposing water reuse and stormwater use. An application for an alternate solution under the Safety Codes
Act must be submitted to the local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) for diverting wastewater or stormwater
and include all details of source, treatment, end-use, operation, maintenance, storage including water
quality. Alternative solutions proposals are reviewed considering the objectives and intent of the plumbing
code. In order to receive an alternate solution, the proposal shall demonstrate that the minimum level of
performance required by Division B in the National Plumbing Code will be met. The National Plumbing
Code of Canada does allow for water and stormwater reuse when used for toilet, urinal flushing, or sub-
surface irrigation (these systems may not require an alternate solution), but still requires the water quality
targets be established by an authority and maintained by the applicant.

Approved alternate solutions are a means to authorize installations, processes, equipment, or actions
that do not meet the strict provisions of existing rules. Alternate solutions are required to provide an equal
or greater level of safety to persons and property than those prescribed under the Safety Codes Act. The
following issues must be addressed and documented in an alternative solutions proposal for water reuse
prior to submitting the application to demonstrate that equal or greater level of safety has been achieved:
     The reclaimed/reused water must be classified as non-potable by a regulatory authority (authorities
        may include but are not limited to the local municipality, an independent laboratory, Alberta
        Environment and Parks, or Alberta Health Services).
     The treatment system must be capable of meeting water quality benchmarks established in the
        prescriptive requirements of the Public Health Guidelines for Water Reuse and Stormwater Use for
        any system that impacts public health.
     Use of the source water may require an approval.
     The local municipality must support the proposed project in principle and accept the proposed
        method of operating and maintaining the system so it is managed satisfactorily. This includes
        monitoring and reporting to the municipality or third party on a regular basis to verify water quality
        benchmarks are being met.
     The plumbing for non-potable water distribution systems must comply with the Plumbing Code and
        other applicable requirements under the Safety Codes Act.

AHJ:

1. Water Quality
    Public Health Guidelines for Water Reuse and Stormwater Use
           https://open.alberta.ca/publications/public-health-guidelines-water-reuse-stormwater-use
    CAN/CSA B128.3 Performance of non-potable water reuse systems

2. Regulations/Regulatory Pathway
    Consult with the local AHJ (i.e. Safety Codes Officer (SCO)), as they are the only entity that can
      consider and issue an alternate solution (variance) from codes identified under the Plumbing Code
      Regulation.
    Consult with a Technical Administrator to apply for a variance with respect to a product or
      equipment for plumbing. An application for an alternate solution to the Technical Administrator
      would apply to deviations of applicable equipment identified within the scope of referenced
      standards within the National Plumbing Code of Canada.
    Consult with the SCO to identify other key reviewers, authorities and ministries that may be
      required.

3. Proposals for Water Reuse on a Single Property Development
   Alternative solutions for water reuse and stormwater use proposals on single properties will require
   a submission to the local AHJ and may require a second submissions to the Technical Administrator
   with Alberta Municipal Affairs for equipment,

     The applicant may also be required to supply notification of approval or exemption from Alberta
     Environmental and Parks (AEP) to divert and use the water.

     An application for Alternative solutions proposals to the AHJ shall contain the following information:
         Project description to characterize the water source, end use and applicable performance
           targets. Where the source is water from a storm drainage treatment facility (i.e., stormwater)
           or wastewater as defined within the Wastewater and Storm Drainage Regulation
           (Environmental Protection and Enhancement Act), authorizations from AEP in accordance with
           the Water Act and/or EPEA may be required.
         Relevant treatment system design specifications and drawings stamped by a professional
           engineer.
         Description of procedures for installation, monitoring, maintenance and, reporting.
         Evidence of collaboration with stakeholders including:
         o Documentation showing the local municipality (accredited or unaccredited) supports the
             proposed water reuse or stormwater project and accepts responsibility to oversee the
             proposed method of operating, monitoring and reporting required for the system so that
             operations continuously meet the water quality benchmarks.
         o In cases where stormwater is used as source water, an Alberta Environment and Park (AEP)
             approval for use of source waters.
         o Feedback from Alberta Health Services based on the Public Health Guidelines.
         o Feedback from AEP.
         Description and identification of a backup system in the event of system failure.

4. Multi-Property Development Proposals
   AEP is the lead approving authority for any water reuse or stormwater system proposal involving more
   than one property. However, the National Plumbing Code still applies to plumbing systems within
   buildings. For such systems within buildings, the applicant must contact both the regional office of AEP
   where the project is located to obtain information relative to the type and extent of the project and the
   AHJ.

     As with a single property, all water reuse and stormwater use projects that fall within the
     prescriptive requirements for the National Plumbing Code of Canada are required to identify and
     maintain a sustainable water quality similar to that required for alternate solutions through evidence
     of compliance with CSA B128.3 or the public health guidelines for water reuse and stormwater use.
