Blatchford, 443 m away. A City-owned network for the Blatchford neighbourhood (planned for up to 30,000 people). Its pipes carry water at only 8–32 °C; every building has its own heat pump and draws heat from the loop. Today the loop is fed by 570 boreholes 150 m deep; sewer heat is in design. more

What the data centre could put in
up to 105.1 GWh a year of server heat through a heat exchanger, with no heat pump at the data centre: even air-cooled heat (25–35 °C) is warmer than the loop more
When it is wanted
Edmonton is below 12–18 °C in 75–89% of the hours of a typical year; June to August carry only 6.5% of the year's heating need, so summer heat still goes to the data centre's own coolers, which keep their full size (CWEC, computed) more
Scale
no actual figures have been published for 2024–2026; the latest public numbers are forecasts made in 2021 and 2023. Small so far: 181 customers forecast for 2024 and 292 for 2025 (Utility Advisor, 2023), over 100 homes connected (operator, 2025); the 2021 plan forecast 3,031 MWh delivered in total from 2020 to 2024 (to check against the original), about 3% of one year of this data centre's heat. Demand grows as the neighbourhood builds out more
Money
customers pay $0.0358 per kWh for loop energy in 2025–26 (frozen by Council), plus fixed charges; no price is published for heat supplied into the loop. The utility ran a forecast revenue shortfall of $1.3 million for 2024. On the data-centre side, no heat pump to buy or run more
Summer
the utility says it already sends surplus summer heat into the borefield for winter (operator statement, no measured data); how much more it could take is not known more
A built case
FGZ Friesenberg, Zürich (2014): a data centre feeds an 8–28 °C network through a heat exchanger, with boreholes as seasonal storage; the warm pipe reaches about 30 °C in summer after the data centre (Buffa et al. 2019, peer-reviewed; the 27 GWh a year is from a factsheet, not stated as measured) more
Not yet known
whether the utility takes heat from an outside source (nothing in the bylaw, plans or the City's 2024 district energy report mentions it); how much the loop can take in winter more
