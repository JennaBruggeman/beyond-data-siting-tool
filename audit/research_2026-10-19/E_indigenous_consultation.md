### When the Crown's duty is triggered (BC)
- Local government: "Local governments do not have a constitutional obligation to consult on the potential impacts" (p.3); "The Province has a legal duty to consult and, where required, accommodate First Nations" (p.1) — Guide to First Nations Engagement on Local Government Statutory Approvals, rev. Dec 2014, interim — https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/local-governments/governance-powers/first_nations_engagement_guide.pdf — **90** (dated; check for a newer edition)
- Province's trigger: knowledge of a claimed or proven right and "a proposed government decision may impact that claimed or proven aboriginal right or treaty right"; "triggered for most government decisions respecting Crown land and resources" (p.9); "the Province may delegate certain procedural aspects of consultation to proponents" (p.4) — Updated Procedures, 7 May 2010, interim — https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/natural-resource-stewardship/consulting-with-first-nations/first-nations/legal_obligations_when_consulting_with_first_nations.pdf — **90**
- Water licence (WSA): "The province's duty to consult First Nations is triggered where it contemplates an action or decision"; "Separate from the duty to consult, First Nations also have the right to notice and to object" (§7.3, p.8) — Water Authorization Application Assessment guidance, eff. 30 Jun 2019 — https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/air-land-water/water/water-licensing-and-rights/water_authorization_assessment_guidance_for_applicants_signed.pdf — **90**
  - Links to `licence_flag`: BC open-loop = WSA licence (already in register).
- BCUC: "it does not have the jurisdiction to engage in consultation or to discharge the Crown's duty to consult" but "does have the jurisdiction to consider the adequacy of Crown consultation"; a CPCN "application should describe the consultation that has been undertaken" — https://www.bcuc.com/AboutUs/IndigenousRelations (undated) — regulator — **90**
- Environmental assessment (Reviewable Projects Reg., B.C. Reg. 243/2019, Table 7): power plant "≥ 50 MW"; transmission line "345 kV or higher voltage and of 40 km or more in length"; groundwater (Table 9 item 4) "≥ 75 litres/second" — https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/243_2019 — regulation — **90**
  - The regulation does not list data centres. A data centre alone would not be reviewable unless it hits one of these rows (**60**, inference).
- BC Hydro 2026 Call for Demand scores First Nations criteria at 10 % — already in register (`bchCfDRfA`) — not re-read here.

### When the Crown's duty is triggered (Alberta)
- "The duty to consult is a constitutional obligation on the Crown."   - Same page: the utilities regulator "requires it to engage Indigenous communities within range of the project."
- First Nations policy applies "When the Crown contemplates a decision on land / natural resource management" with potential adverse impacts on Treaty rights or traditional uses. Policies: First Nations 2013; Metis Settlements 2015. ACO determines "whether consultation is required" and assesses adequacy. — https://www.alberta.ca/indigenous-consultations-in-alberta — **90**
- "The ACO is responsible for deciding the adequacy of consultation for activities requiring AER approvals." For AUC/NRCB: "Alberta relies on the tribunal's process to address potential adverse impacts." — https://www.alberta.ca/proponent-led-indigenous-consultations — **90**
- AUC Rule 007 (effective 6 Nov 2025): thermal plant TP35 — where Alberta "through the Aboriginal Consultation Office (ACO)" directed consultation, applicant files "the pre-consultation assessment, the adequacy assessment and the specific issues and response table"; if no advice sought, "justify not seeking it"; TP36 participant involvement per "Appendix A1-B – Participant involvement program guidelines for Indigenous groups". Exemption: power plant "less than one MW" may proceed without application (s.4.1.2). — https://media.auc.ab.ca/prd-wp-uploads/regulatory_documents/Consultations/Rule007.pdf — regulator rule — **90**
- AUC: an Indigenous group asserting s.35 rights "can submit a statement of intent to participate in the proceeding." — https://www.auc.ab.ca/duty-to-consult/ — **90**

### Corrections first
- **AUC Rule 007, Indigenous involvement threshold: CORRECTED.** Round 1 rule sketch "on-site gen ≥ 1 MW → AUC Rule 007 Indigenous participant involvement" is too broad.
  - AUC "Guidance for proponents", Step 1 (PIP), Consultation Category B: "Small (e.g., thermal power plants less than 10 megawatts)." and "On private land with limited to no off-site impacts (e.g., transmission lines, solar, wind)." → "Indigenous groups not ordinarily included in the PIP."
  - Category A: Crown has found a duty to consult for related approvals (Water Act, Public Lands Act, EPEA), or a First Nation reserve or Métis Settlement is inside the notification radius → Indigenous groups must be in the PIP. *(paraphrase from fetch, not verbatim)*
  - Category C: "Application category will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis to determine whether the Indigenous groups should be included in the PIP."

  - https://www.auc.ab.ca/guidance-for-proponents/ (undated) — regulator guidance — **90**
