### 1.2 Storage tanks inside buildings — NFC Part 4, Subsection 4.3.13 (text as in BCFC 2018 / NFC(AE) 2019)

- **Definition (Div. A 1.4.1.2.)**: storage tank = "a vessel for flammable liquids or combustible liquids having a capacity of more than 230 L". **VERIFIED** [S2], [S6]. (Containers ≤ 230 L are not "storage tanks"; they fall under container storage rules, not researched here.)
- **4.3.13.1.(1) Occupancy**: indoor tanks are permitted in industrial occupancies and in any occupancy where combustible liquids are stored as fuel for "oil-burning equipment, emergency generators and fire pumps". **VERIFIED** [S1], [S2].
- **4.3.13.2.(1)–(2)**: "Storage tank systems using Class I liquids as fuel supplies for stationary engines inside buildings shall conform to this Part when they are used to supply appliances installed in conformance with NFPA 37"; Class II and IIIA tank systems conform to Part 4 when supplying appliances installed under CSA B139. **VERIFIED** [S2]. (Diesel is normally a Class II combustible liquid — this classification is **UNVERIFIED** here; the flash-point definitions in Div. A are: combustible ≥ 37.8 °C and < 93.3 °C [S6].)
- **4.3.13.3**: static head at tank bottom ≤ 70 kPa (gauge) with vent/fill pipe full, unless tank designed for more. **VERIFIED** [S1], [S3].
- **4.3.13.4.(1)**: "Except as provided in Subsection 4.2.8. and in Sentence (2), storage tanks … shall be a) located in dedicated storage rooms conforming to Subsection 4.3.14., and b) located in conformance with Tables 4.3.13.4.A. and 4.3.13.4.B." **VERIFIED** [S2].
- **4.3.13.4.(2)**: tanks may be outside a storage room for special process needs only if total per fire compartment ≤ ½ of Table A, **on the first storey**, and Articles 4.3.13.9–4.3.13.12 and 4.3.14.4 are met. NRC intent: first storey because there "manual firefighting operations are not as likely to be delayed or impeded". **VERIFIED** [S1], [S3], [S10].
- **4.3.13.5**: tanks > 2 500 L and ≤ 20 000 L used under Table B must be double-walled (4.3.1.2.(1)(e)) or have secondary containment, with leak monitoring (4.4.2.1.(7)); tanks > 20 000 L must conform to 4.3.1.2.(1)(l). **VERIFIED** (wording partly garbled in extraction) [S1].
- **4.3.13.6.(1)**: "Piping systems serving oil-burning equipment, diesel-engine-driven emergency generators and fire pumps" shall conform to CSA B139. **VERIFIED** [S1].
- **4.3.13.7**: fire compartments separated by ≥ 2 h fire separation. **VERIFIED** [S1].
- **4.3.13.9–4.3.13.12**: outside a storage room — 100 % spill containment of largest tank or drainage, ventilation; venting per 4.3.4/4.3.5 (weak roof-to-shell seam not acceptable indoors); supports per 4.3.3; bonding and grounding. **VERIFIED** [S1], [S3].

Appendix note A-4.3.13.4.(1)(b) (as quoted by Alberta STANDATA): CSA B139 "limits the capacity of individual storage tanks to 2 500 L and their aggregate capacity to 5 000 L". **VERIFIED as quoted in [S2]**; the CSA public summary places these limits in **CSA B139.2** (residential/small commercial: individual ≤ 2 500 L, total 5 000 L per premises) [S12].

Day tanks: **none of the code texts read mentions "day tank"**. Treatment of a generator day tank vs. a main storage tank under 4.3.13 is **UNVERIFIED** — ask the AHJ / read CSA B139.1.1 and CSA C282 Clause 7.3.

**Applicability caveat (UNVERIFIED):** Table B and 4.3.13.1 name "emergency generators". Whether a data-centre *standby* (non-life-safety) generator is treated the same way is an interpretation question not answered by any source read. Alberta STANDATA says tanks supplying "stationary combustion engines, internal combustion engines and oil burning equipment shall comply with the requirements of Part 4 of the NFC(AE)" [S2], which suggests Part 4 applies either way.

### 1.3 Storage tank rooms — 4.3.14 — **VERIFIED** [S1], [S2], [S3]
- 4.3.14.1.(1): room "a) separated from the rest of the building by a fire separation having a fire-resistance rating of at least 2 h, b) designed to contain a spill equal to at least 100% of the volume of the largest storage tank, c) made liquid-tight where the walls join the floor, and d) used for no other purposes than the storage and handling of flammable liquids or combustible liquids." [S2]
- 4.3.14.2: ≥ 550 mm clearance between room walls and tank sides. [S1], [S3]
- 4.3.14.3: explosion venting (NFPA 68) where Class IA/IB liquids are dispensed. [S1]
- 4.3.14.4: hose stations if no standpipe required; Class B extinguishers. 4.3.14.5: placards and fire safety plan entry. [S1]
- Alberta interpretation: "it is the storage tank/storage tank system supplying the fuel that dictates the rating" (i.e. the 2 h). [S2]

### 1.4 Generator room — NBC/VBBL 3.6.2.8 and CSA C282
- VBBL 2019 3.6.2.8.(1): where a generator "intended to supply emergency power for lighting, fire safety and life safety systems" is in a building (unless the building only houses the generator), it shall be in a room that "(a) is separated from the remainder of the building by a fire separation having a fire-resistance rating not less than 2 h, and (b) contains only the generating set and equipment related to the emergency power supply system." **VERIFIED** [S13]. Same wording in NBC(AE) 3.6.2.8.(1) as quoted in [S2].
- City of Vancouver Bulletin 2000-015-BU/EL (rev. 2015, attachment rev. 2020): CSA C282 requires "separate service room(s) used solely for the generator sets and all auxiliary equipment"; auxiliary equipment includes "the engine, generator, fuel supply, starting batteries, instrumentation, switchgear"; fuel system tanks and piping per CSA C282 Clause 7.3 (not reproduced). An outdoor prefabricated enclosure is an option if "fire or spatially-separated". No litre limits, no basement/roof rule. **VERIFIED** [S14].
- VBBL 3.6.2.1.(1): fuel-fired appliances in service rooms with ≥ 1 h fire separation (exceptions); 3.6.1.5.(1) covers fuel-fired appliances on roofs/outside (text truncated). **VERIFIED (partial)** [S13].
- Calgary BST plan requirements (NBC(AE)/CSA C282-09): in a high building, where a generator room "has a supply of fuel less than 2 hours", fuel piping, pumps, valves, wiring and controls outside the room must be protected by a 2 h assembly (NBC(AE) 3.2.7.8). **VERIFIED as stated by City of Calgary** [S7]; the NBC clause itself **NOT READ**.
- Calgary and Red Deer: CSA B139 is intended for factory self-contained units with attached tanks; separate tanks needing site piping must meet NBC(AE)/NFC(AE). All tanks > 230 L need approval before installation (Calgary). **VERIFIED as municipal guidance** [S7], [S15].

### 1.6 NFPA 37 / NFPA 30 / NFPA 110 (public statements only)
NFC 4.3.13.2 references NFPA 37 for **Class I** fuels only [S2]. Public statements found are from a US state agency checklist (California HCAI/OSHPD, 2025) [S16]: NFPA 37 §6.3.2.1 "Fuel tanks inside structures shall be securely mounted on noncombustible supports"; NFPA 110 §7.9.12 listed generator sub-base containment tanks of 660 gal and below may be installed without diking; NFPA 30 §24.5.1 (inferred by the source) spill control/secondary containment for tanks "inside structure or on roof of structure". Gallon caps in that checklist (660 / 1 320 / 3 000 gal) are **California Fire Code**, not NFPA, and **do not apply in Canada**. Use for context only.

## 2. Structure-borne noise and vibration to adjacent units

### 2.1 Code requirements (airborne sound only)
- VBBL 2019 5.8.1.1.(1)(a): dwelling units separated from noise-generating spaces by assemblies giving "an apparent sound transmission class (ASTC) rating not less than 47"; (b) alternative STC ≥ 50 with flanking constructions per 9.11.1.4. **VERIFIED** [S17].
- VBBL 2019 5.8.1.1.(2): next to an elevator shaft or refuse chute, "shall have an STC rating not less than 55". **VERIFIED** [S17]. NRC intent: elevators and chutes "are expected to generate relatively high noise levels" [S18].
- BCBC 2018 9.11.1.1.(1) and (3): same ASTC 47 / STC 50 / STC 55 values for Part 9 buildings. **VERIFIED** [S19].
- NBC 2020, BCBC 2024, NBC(AE) 2023 equivalents: **UNVERIFIED** (not opened; values assumed unchanged only for planning).
- **What the code does not cover:** VBBL 5.8 does not address noise or vibration from mechanical equipment; its only vibration reference is the "vibration reduction index" used in flanking calculations [S17]. BCBC 9.11 does not mention mechanical equipment or vibration [S19]. Peer-reviewed note (Ontario): the building code "does not require vibration isolation for equipment supported from the structure" (paraphrase) [S20]. Whether NBC 2020 has any equipment-vibration requirement elsewhere: **UNVERIFIED (none found)**. ASTC/STC are single-number airborne ratings and do not quantify low-frequency structure-borne transmission from machinery.

### 2.2 Mechanisms and isolation approaches (authoritative guidance)
- **Primary reference, NOT READ:** ASHRAE Handbook — HVAC Applications (2023), Chapter 49 "Noise and Vibration Control" (TC 2.6). Chapter number **VERIFIED** from ASHRAE table of contents [S21]; content not read. Chapter 20 is "Data Centers and Telecommunication Facilities" [S21].
- **US VA guide spec 23 05 41 (cites the ASHRAE HVAC Applications Noise and Vibration Control chapter)** — **VERIFIED** [S22]:
  - Isolator selection table by equipment and **support location: on grade, 20 ft, 30 ft, 40 ft, 50 ft floor span**. Required minimum static deflection is lowest on grade and rises with span; e.g. packaged refrigeration machines: on grade D-type 0.25, 20–30 ft span spring 1.5, 40 ft 2.5, 50 ft spring + rails 2.5 (units not printed in the extraction; inches assumed — UNVERIFIED). Floor-mounted AHUs: 0.75 on grade vs 2.5–3.5 on spans. Roof ventilators "above occupied areas" need spring/curb isolation.
  - Note: for suspended floors thinner than 100 mm concrete, use the deflection of the next longer span.
  - Inertia bases ≥ equipment weight; flexible connections for piping, conduit and duct; first three hangers isolated.
  - Chillers (other than "packaged refrigeration machines"), cooling towers, generators and transformers are **not** in that table.
- **Sandia spec 15070S (US DOE lab, 2003)**: engine-driven generators on isolation bases with spring isolators; transformers > 200 kVA on ribbed/waffle neoprene pads ≥ 2 in thick, natural frequency ≤ 12 Hz; centrifugal chillers treated differently on slab-on-grade vs elsewhere. **VERIFIED as project specification, not general guidance** [S23].
- **Peer-reviewed / proceedings:**
  - Mak & Su (2001), J. Low Frequency Noise, Vibration and Active Control 20(1):1–11: isolator efficiency depends on floor mobility, which "is usually ignored by engineers". Abstract only read. [S24]
  - Bailhache, Dépierre & Picard (2017), ICSV24: isolators are "less adapted for installation in a lightweight building"; predicted (not measured) results. Designers should "consider the dynamic behavior of the equipment but also of the supporting structure". [S25]
  - Melo & Chapnik (2001), Canadian Acoustics 29(3):84–85: pumps, chillers and boilers are vibration sources in multi-tenant buildings; rigid pipe supports make structures vibrate; remedies are neoprene pads, springs, flexible connections, resilient pipe mounts, balancing; retrofits after complaints are costly. [S20]
  - Anda (2025), Forum Acusticum/EAA: for a 10 t diesel genset, a second elastic stage "significantly improves isolation"; heavier base frame lowers natural frequency. Marine/industrial focus. [S26]

### 2.3 Which floors are worst/best (from the sources, not modelled)
- Slab-on-grade (basement or ground floor on grade) needs the least isolator deflection; suspended floors need more as span increases [S22]. Lighter/more mobile supporting structure reduces isolator effectiveness [S24], [S25].
- Code airborne ratings (ASTC 47 / STC 55) apply between dwelling units and any adjoining space, regardless of floor [S17].
- No source gives dB values for floor position. **Do not put dB numbers in the tool.**
