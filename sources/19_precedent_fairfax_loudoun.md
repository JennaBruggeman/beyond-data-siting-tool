# 19 — Precedent: Fairfax County and Loudoun County (Virginia) data centre zoning

Research date: 2026-10-14. Prepared by Claude for the "Beyond Data" tool (GP1 / ARCH 540).
Spelling: Canadian in my text; US ordinance text quoted verbatim.

**Method and limits.** All rules below come from county government documents (adopted ordinance text, staff reports, county project pages, county handouts). News, law-firm and industry pages appeared in search results but were used only to locate official documents and are not cited. Quotes were obtained through a fetch tool that returns quotes in fragments of under 125 characters; where a sentence is split, the pieces are joined with "…". The Fairfax adopted-text PDF is an OCR scan ("I-2" prints as "1-2"); district labels are corrected in brackets where obvious. Anything I could not confirm against an official text is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

## 2. Fairfax County

### 2.1 Definition (as a use)

- The adopted amendment contains **no definition** of "Data Center"; it amends the use tables, the use-specific standards at 4102.6.A, and submission requirements only [S1]. The 2024 staff report also does not quote a Zoning Ordinance definition [S2].
- In the adopted text the standards sit under "6. Industrial Uses" → "Freight Movement, Warehousing, and Wholesale Distribution" → "A. Data Center" [S1]. (So the use is classed as industrial.)
- County research report, descriptive only: "A data center is a facility that houses computer servers and their network connections." [S4, pp. 2–3]
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether Article 9 of the Fairfax Zoning Ordinance carries a formal definition. Not retrieved.
- Note: the county's "Noise Study Requirements" sheet cites "Subsection 4102.7.A(6)" [S8], while the adopted text says 4102.6.A [S1]. I use 4102.6.A (adopted text); the discrepancy is unresolved.

### 2.2 Districts and permission type

Adopted text [S1]:
- "revise the permissions for Data Center from P to P or SE in [I-4, I-5, I-6]" (OCR prints "1-4,1-5,1-6").
- Planned districts: "remove the permission for a data center in the PRC District, and change the PDC and PTC permissions to SE."
- (2) "In the C-3 and C-4 Districts, the maximum building size is 40,000 square feet of gross floor area." … "However, this size limit may be exceeded with special exception approval in accordance with subsection 8100.3."
- (3) "In the [I-2, I-3, and I-4] Districts, the maximum building size is 80,000 square feet of gross floor area." … same SE sentence.

Staff report (17 May 2024), prior vs proposed [S2, pp. 3–4, 10–11]: before the amendment, C-3/C-4 were by right to 40,000 sf, I-2/I-3 by right to 80,000 sf, I-4/I-5/I-6 by right limited by height (75 ft in I-4) and FAR; PDC/PTC permitted if shown on a development plan; PRC permitted in Town Center and Convention/Conference Center areas.

**Reading (Claude's):** by right only within the size caps in C-3, C-4, I-2, I-3, I-4; by right in I-5 and I-6 with no added cap; SE above caps or in PDC/PTC; not permitted in PRC. The use-table cells in the OCR scan are garbled, so the exact cell values for C-3/C-4/I-2/I-3 are **UNVERIFIED** against the table itself.

### 2.3 Use-specific standards — 4102.6.A [S1]

*Applies to all data centres*

- **(1) Equipment enclosure / substation.** Cooling, ventilating and power equipment "must be fully enclosed, except where determined by the Director not to be mechanically feasible"; if not feasible, a wall. Equipment "If located on the ground, any equipment for cooling, ventilating, or otherwise operating the facility" includes "any power generator or other power supply equipment." Accessory electrical substation: "must be screened from view from abutting lots and from rights-of-way by a visually solid wall or a building."
- **(4)(a) Building distance.** "Any data center building must be located at least 200 feet from the lot line of an R district" … "or a property developed with a residential use."
- **(4)(b) Ground equipment.** Must be "Located at least 300 feet from the lot line of an R district or a property developed with a residential use" or "Separated from the lot line of an R district or a property developed with a residential use" by the principal building.
- **(4)(c)** "Lesser distances may be allowed with special exception approval in accordance with subsection 8100.3."
- **(4)(d)** "an R district does not include an area within a public street right-of-way."
- **(5) Metro.** "A data center building must be located at least one mile from a Metro station entrance." … "A lesser distance may be allowed with special exception approval in accordance with subsection 8100.3."
- **(6) Noise.** "Prior to site plan approval, a noise study must be submitted" and "prior to issuance of a Nonresidential Use Permit, a post-construction noise study must be submitted" — demonstrating compliance with Chapter 108.1.

*When permitted by right*

- **(7) Entrance.** "The entrance feature must also either project or recess from the adjoining building plane."
- **(8)(a) Façade.** "A change in the façade surface for every 150 horizontal feet" (material, pattern, texture, colour or accent).
- **(8)(b) Fenestration.** Must "comprise a minimum of 30 percent of the individual façade."

*When permitted by development plan or SE*

- "A data center building must be designed to minimize adverse visual impacts on surrounding development"; within 200 ft of an R district or residential property it "should include changes in building height or other design techniques to provide variation in building mass."

*Submission requirements (8101.2.E(12), 8101.3.D(8))*: "A noise study demonstrating that the operation of the data center will comply with the Noise Ordinance" and "Architectural depictions of the proposed building and associated equipment as viewed from all lot lines and street lines."

*Not in 4102.6.A:* a data-centre-specific height limit (district height limits apply; rooftop equipment over 25% of roof area counts toward height — staff report [S2, pp. 4–5]), landscaping beyond the screening wall, lighting, energy or water conditions.

### 2.4 Noise — how measured

Fairfax Noise Ordinance, Chapter 108.1 (adopted 17 Nov 2015, effective 17 Feb 2016) [S5]; Sect. 108.1-4-2(a). Measured "at the property boundary of the sound source or at any point within any other property affected by the sound."

| Receiving area | 7 a.m.–10 p.m. | 10 p.m.–7 a.m. |
|---|---|---|
| Residential areas in residential districts | 60 dBA | 55 dBA |
| Non-residential areas in residential districts | 60 dBA | 60 dBA |
| Mixed-use areas | 65 dBA | 60 dBA |
| Commercial districts | 65 dBA | 65 dBA |
| Industrial districts | 72 dBA | 65 dBA |

(Values cross-checked against Table 2 of the county research report [S4, p. 16].) Impulse limits (dB) also exist; not used here.

**Generator testing**, Sect. 108.1-5-1(g)(2): routine testing allowed only 7 a.m.–9 p.m. and "shall not occur for more than two (2) consecutive or non-consecutive hours in any one (1) day" [S5].

Noise study sheet (Fall 2024) [S8]: modelling must address "expected maximum noise output with all cooling and any other noise-generating equipment operating simultaneously"; prepared by a licensed PE specialising in acoustics or INCE Board Certified engineer; post-construction field measurements at full load. No metric beyond the Ordinance's maximum sound levels is specified (no dBC/low-frequency rule). Staff found no basis for different dBA limits for data centres and suggested further study of low-frequency standards (dBC) [S4, pp. 18, 22].

### 2.5 Reasons stated

- Staff report: to address "compatibility with surrounding uses, noise, and aesthetics" [S2, p. 3]; community feedback on energy demand, noise, aesthetics, proximity to homes [S2, p. 9]; data centres "would generally not fit" transit-area development (Metro distance advertised from ½ mile up to 1 mile; 1 mile adopted) [S2, pp. 6–7, 12].
- Matters staff said were **outside zoning authority**: energy (State Corporation Commission), generator air emissions (VDEQ), sodium in cooling-water discharge, and any change to generator exemptions (needs a Noise Ordinance amendment) [S2, pp. 7–9].
- Research report: data centres "can affect surrounding uses, environmental resources, and infrastructure"; noise studies for data centre projects showed mitigation was needed to meet the Noise Ordinance [S4, pp. 5, 17]. Grid context: a ~400,000 sf facility ≈ 48–60 MW (stated as staff estimate); typical substation ~300 MW [S4, pp. 7–8].
- County news release (11 Sep 2024): responds to concerns about noise, design and proximity to homes [S3].
- Follow-on motions [S9]: report back in 12 months on energy-efficiency/LEED commitments; Comprehensive Plan guidance on water, air, noise; monitor wastewater/salinisation; present substation information; report on absorption of industrial land.

### 2.6 Process dates

Board research request 9 May 2023 → research report 9 Jan 2024 [S4] → Board direction 19 Mar 2024 → staff report 17 May 2024; PC hearing 5 Jun 2024 [S2] → adopted 10 Sep 2024, effective 11 Sep 2024 [S1, S3, S6]. Substation amendment adopted 9 Dec 2025 (setback, screening and noise-study standards; distances **UNVERIFIED** — adopted text not readable) [S7].

## 3. Loudoun County

### 3.1 Definition

- **UNVERIFIED:** the in-force Zoning Ordinance definition of "Data Center" (Chapter 13 or equivalent) — not retrieved; neither the redline nor the clean Phase 2 draft I could read included it [S14, S15].
- Phase 2 handout gives a **proposed** definition, "subject to Board approval": "A secure facility, building or a group of facilities used primarily to house computing" … (text continues; full wording not retrieved) [S16].
- History (county fact sheet): around 2000 the Zoning Administrator classed data centres as similar to offices and allowed them by right wherever offices were allowed [S18].

### 3.2 Districts and permission type

- Phase 1 (ZOAM-2024-0001), approved 18 Mar 2025: "Requires Special Exception (SPEX) approval for data center uses in the Industrial Park (IP), General Industry (GI), and Mineral Resources-Heavy Industry (MR-HI) districts" (county paraphrase) [S10]. The county describes this as eliminating "data centers as a 'by-right' use in the county" [S13].
- Current use table 3.02.05-1 (footer "ZOAM-2024-0001, 03/18/2025"): Data Center = **S** in OP, IP, GI, MR-HI; blank (not permitted) in urban/suburban (TRC, UE, SN, SCN, TC, CC) and rural/transition/JLMA tables [S14, S15]. Legacy suburban table shows two "S" cells whose columns could not be mapped — **UNVERIFIED**.
- 4.06.02.B: "data centers may only be permitted by SPEX pursuant to Sections 10.11.01 and 10.11.11," unless Section 1.02.L applies [S14].
- Rural policy area (about two-thirds of the county): districts "have not and do not allow data center development" [S13].
- **Grandfathering** (Application Pathway Guide, illustrative; resolution itself was an unreadable scan) [S19]: legislative applications accepted by 12 Feb 2025 continue; administrative applications qualify only if accepted by 12 Feb 2025, by right at acceptance, and the structure and external mechanical equipment are **more than 500 ft (152 m) from existing residential structures**.

### 3.3 Use-specific standards — Section 4.06.02

**Status caveat.** I could not open the in-force online ordinance (blocked, see §6). The text below is the base text shown in the county's Phase 2 redline and clean drafts (Sep 2026) [S14, S15]. The county's Phase 2 summary says it would remove the façade interval requirement, green walls, and the height change / step-back standards [S20] — which indicates those are in force now. For the other items (200 ft setback, generator hours, berm), in-force status is **UNVERIFIED** (redline markup was lost in extraction). The county fact sheet confirms the 2023 Zoning Ordinance "strengthened design requirements" [S18].

- **Setback (G.7.b):** "Structures must be setback at least 200 feet from the common property line" for property with residential use, approval or zoning.
- **Parking (G.7.a):** ≥ 50 ft (15.2 m) from property line where existing forest screens it; otherwise berms ≥ 10 ft (3.0 m) at max 2:1 slope with 10 ft fencing and plantings.
- **Equipment location (G.2.b, G.2.c):** "a principal building must be located between ground mounted Data Center Equipment and any adjacent property"; equipment "must not be located in any required front yard or setback."
- **Equipment screening (G.2):** mechanical yards "must be fully screened on all sides" (visually solid; perforation allowed for ventilation); may be waived beside IP, GI, MR-HI property but not from public roads.
- **Massing within 400 ft (122 m) of residential (G.7.c, G.7.d):** height change "no less frequent than every 150 horizontal linear feet"; "a step-back of no less than 15 feet from the building wall" from the second storey or 40 ft (12.2 m), whichever is lower. *Proposed for removal in Phase 2* [S20].
- **Façade (F.1–F.4):** intervals "no less frequent than every 150 horizontal linear feet"; "Fenestration must comprise at least 30% of the total surface coverage area of the Principal Façade"; green walls may replace up to half; foundation plantings ≥ 50% of façade length.
- **Loading (G.1):** "Loading bays are permitted to be located on only 1 façade."
- **Landscape (G.11):** Buffer Type C on an earthen berm "that has a minimum height of 6 feet and a grade lower than 2:1"; evergreens 40–70% of plant units, ≥ 8 ft (2.4 m) at planting.
- **Noise (G.10):** "Data Centers are subject to the Noise Study standards pursuant to Section 7.05.03.G"; equipment next to residential "screened on all four sides by an acoustical barrier."
- **Generator testing (G.8):** "Generator testing is limited to between 5:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. between May 1 and September 30;" and "Generator testing is limited to between 11:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. between October 1 and April 30; and" otherwise backup/emergency only. (No reason stated in what I read; inferring a link to summer peak electrical demand would be an assumption — **UNVERIFIED**.)
- **Lighting (G.9):** "Data Centers must include a photometric plan that shows all exterior lighting"; maximum illumination per Section 7.05.02.B.3 (values not retrieved).
- **Utilities (G.4):** "subject to Utilities requirements pursuant to Section 7.08" (content not retrieved).
- **Height:** no data-centre-specific height limit found in 4.06.02.
- **Transit:** no transit-proximity rule found.

### 3.4 Noise — how measured

- County web page: maximum **55 dBA at the receiving property line** for residential properties; no day/night split stated; ordinance section not given [S17]. The text of Zoning Ordinance Section 7.05.03 was **not retrieved** — values, hours and metrics are **UNVERIFIED** against the ordinance.
- Post-construction study "no earlier than one month and no later than 12 months after the first Certificate of Occupancy" [S17].
- No tonal or low-frequency rule yet; amendments "anticipated" spring/summer 2027 [S17]. Phase 2 proposes LFN and tonal measurement methods and periodic post-construction studies [S21].
- Separate County Codified Ordinance 654.02 (nuisance noise "audible and discernible" inside a dwelling, enforced from 1 Sep 2016) has no dBA values [S22].

### 3.5 Phase 2 (proposed, not adopted) — numeric items [S20]

- Substation setback from residential: **500 ft (152 m) air-insulated (AIS); 200 ft (61 m) gas-insulated (GIS)**, replacing 100 ft (30.5 m) (4.07.09); AIS and hybrid need SPEX, GIS minor SPEX.
- Substation opaque wall/fence: **min 20 ft (6.1 m)** (4.07.09).
- On-site power generation: ≥ 200 ft (61 m) from residential use; 2 acres (0.81 ha) per 100 MW minimum area (4.07.12); limited on-site generation in IP and OP.
- Noise assessment radius: residences, schools and noise-sensitive uses within 500 ft (152 m) (CE Policy 7, Comprehensive Plan).
- Site assessments (groundwater, surface water, parks, historic sites, forest) consistent with Va. Code § 15.2-2209.5; discourages on-site wells for non-irrigation use; no numeric water or energy limits.
- Number discrepancy: the summary calls the package ZOAM-2025-0002 / CPAM-2025-0001 [S20]; the project page does not give Phase 2 numbers [S11]. **UNVERIFIED** which is correct.

### 3.6 Reasons stated

- Phase 2 summary: SPEX "allows proposals to be reviewed on case-by-case basis" with a public hearing; Board directed staff to "reduce potential impacts from data centers and related uses on surrounding communities" [S20].
- County land-use page: the county "does not have the authority" to impose a moratorium; it has called for undergrounding of transmission within 500 ft of residences or schools but lacks authority to require it — the State Corporation Commission approves routes [S13].
- Air quality is regulated by VDEQ; Tier 4-equivalent standards for new/modified data centre generators from 1 Jul 2026 [S17].
- Phase 1 staff reports were blocked (see §6), so the reasons recorded at adoption are **UNVERIFIED** beyond the above.

### 3.7 Process dates [S10, S11]

Initiated 6 Feb 2024 → intent to amend 2 Jul 2024 → PC hearing 24 Sep 2024 → Board hearing 12 Feb 2025 (also grandfathering cut-off) → **approved 18 Mar 2025** → Phase 2 plan approved 16 Sep 2025 → substation white paper 7 Jul 2026 → open houses Sep 2026 → (targets) ZOC Oct 2026, Board check-in Dec 2026, PC hearing Feb 2027, Board hearing Jul 2027.
