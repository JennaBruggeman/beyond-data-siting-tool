# 21 — Precedent: City of Calgary, Rocky View County, Airdrie and Alberta-level rules for data centres

Compiled 14 October 2026 for the "Beyond Data" tool (UBC M.Arch). Only official sources are used as sources of truth: Calgary Land Use Bylaw 1P2007 (City online version), City of Calgary draft Zoning Bylaw materials, Rocky View County bylaws, plans and news releases, the City of Airdrie Land Use Bylaw, AUC decisions and filings, AESO documents, and Government of Alberta pages. News items were used only to find documents. Where a news item is the only source for a claim, the claim is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

Caution from the source: the City of Calgary's online bylaw states that it "has no legal status" and is not an official interpretation. The legal consolidated PDF was blocked (see Gaps).

## 1. How Calgary's Land Use Bylaw 1P2007 classifies a data centre

**Finding: there is no named use.** The defined uses in Part 4, Division 2 were read letter by letter on the City's online bylaw: A (ss. 135–151), D (ss. 180–187), G (s. 203.x), I (ss. 216–218.1), O (ss. 246–248), P (to s. 266), T (ss. 316–320) and V (ss. 323–330). None mentions data, servers, computers or information processing. Under D, the uses run from Dinner Theatre to Duplex Dwelling, with no data use. [S1–S6]

The candidate uses, quoted from the bylaw:

- **General Industrial — Light, s. 203.2** [S3]: "a use: (i) where any of the following activities occur: (A) the manufacturing, fabricating, processing, assembly or disassembly of materials, semi-finished goods, finished goods, food, beverages, products or equipment … (B) the cleaning, servicing, testing, repairing or maintenance of industrial or commercial goods and equipment; … (G) research and development; or (H) the repair, service or refurbishment of furniture, electronic equipment and appliances that are used in the home; … (iii) where all of the processes and functions associated with the use are contained within a fully enclosed building; (iv) where no dust or vibration is seen or felt outside of the building containing the use". (b) "is a use within the General Industrial Group in Schedule A". (f) "requires a minimum of 1.0 bicycle parking stalls — class 2 per 2000.0 square metres of gross usable floor area."
  - **Fit:** Claude's interpretation, **UNVERIFIED**. The list of activities does not describe housing and operating computing equipment. "Processing" refers to materials and goods, not data. The enclosed-building and no-vibration conditions fit a data hall. Section 134.1(1) "deems" certain listed uses to be GI-Light in certain districts (the list was not retrieved) [S2].
- **General Industrial — Medium, s. 203.3** [S3]: same activities, but "part of the processes and functions … may be located outside of a building" and "dust or vibration may be seen or felt outside of the building … provided it is contained on the parcel". This could cover outdoor chillers or generator yards. **Interpretation, UNVERIFIED.**
- **Information and Service Provider, s. 216** [S4]: "where services, expertise or access to information, other than professional services, are provided to a broad spectrum of the public without appointment; … may have a counter where the clients may take a number or wait in a line to be served". It is in the **Sales Group**. **This does not fit a data centre.** It is a walk-in public service use.
- **Office, s. 246** [S5]: "where business people, professional, clerical and administrative staff work …; that provides services to either a select clientele or no clients". It is in the Office Group. It covers staffed workspace, not equipment halls. **Interpretation.**
- **Utilities / Utilities – Linear (s. 321) and Utility Building (s. 322)**: these definitions were **not retrieved** (see Gaps). s. 134(1)(c) lists "Utilities — Linear" as permitted in all districts [S2].
- **Power Generation Facility** (relevant to backup or prime generators) [S6]:
  - **Large, s. 264**: "where the total power generation capacity is 12.5 megawatts or greater" and "must be approved only on a parcel designated as a Direct Control District". Parking is set by a parking study.
  - **Medium, s. 265**: 1.0–12.5 MW. It "must not be located within 50.0 metres of a residential district", "must be located within a building, with the exception of solar collectors", "must be shielded and insulated so as to limit noise generation as much as possible", "must be screened", and "does not require motor vehicle parking stalls".
  - **Small, s. 266**: 10 W to 1.0 MW.
  - Whether standby generators inside a data centre count as a PGF *use* under 1P2007 is **UNVERIFIED**. The AUC licenses them as power plants (§4).

**Draft successor bylaw:** the City is preparing a new "Zoning Bylaw" (draft v1.5, May 2025). The next milestone, "Draft Zoning Bylaw public release and engagement", is "Anticipated for 2027" [S8]. Its draft use matrix has **no data centre use**. Its industrial uses are Crematorium, Heavy Industrial, Industrial, Outdoor Storage and Small-Scale Manufacturing. Major Utility and Minor Utility are listed under Institutional [S9]. The proposed industrial zones are I-F (Flex), I-G (General) and I-H (Heavy). The summary sheet sets "a minimum of 20% mature tree canopy coverage in setback areas" and gives no FAR or height figures [S10].

**General industrial rules, Part 8, Div. 1** [S11]: outdoor "mechanical systems or equipment … shall be positioned, camouflaged or screened" from public spaces and residential parcels within 30 m, judged against a 1.7 m line of sight (902(1)). A 2.0 m screening fence is required where a lane separates the parcel from residential, commercial or special-purpose districts (904(1)). At least 25 % of required trees must be coniferous (899(2)). Soft landscaping needs underground irrigation unless a low-water system is used (898(4)). **No noise rule was found in Part 8.** Noise for generators comes through PGF-Medium s. 265(e) and, provincially, AUC Rule 012 (§4).

**Parking and loading** [S19]: loading is "1.0 loading stalls per 9300.0 square metres of gross floor area" where GFA is over 930 m², and 0 at or below 930 m² (123(5)). Motor-vehicle parking minimums are set in Part 4 and in the districts (118(1)). In the GI-Light, GI-Medium and Office definitions the former parking clauses read "deleted" (Office: amended by 67P2008 and 48P2020) [S3, S5]. **Whether these uses now have a zero motor-vehicle minimum is UNVERIFIED.** Bicycle: GI uses need 1 class-2 stall per 2,000 m² (203.2(f)).

## 4. Calgary policy, council direction and data-centre-specific DC bylaws

- **No Calgary council report, notice of motion, policy, economic strategy section or heat-recovery policy naming data centres was found** on calgary.ca. Searches of the City site and its eScribe pages for "data centre" returned only third-party listings. Calgary Economic Development is an arm's-length agency, and no official Calgary page on data centres was found. This is an absence within what could be searched, not proof that none exists.
- **Direct Control bylaws:** none naming a data centre was found. Three DC bylaws that came up in searches were opened and none mentions data centres. **50D2018** is a CC-X/CC-MHX heritage density transfer. **134D2019** is based on I-B, with FAR 1.1 and height 16 m (40 m in a Schedule B area), and lists PGF-Medium as D; its purpose is "employment-intensive uses". **166D2021** adds Office to CC-MH [S20–S22]. They are not data centre precedents.
- **Calgary data centres in AUC records** (generators):
  - **eStruxture CAL-1, 7007 54th Street S.E., Calgary.** AUC Decision **27603-D01-2022** (4 Oct 2022) approved expanding the standby diesel plant from **9 MW to 12 MW**: three existing 3 MW MTU 20V4000DS3000 units plus one new 3 MW KOHLER KD3000. The AUC accepted Rule 012 compliance because the units run only in outages and monthly tests, the area is industrial, and "there are no residences within 1.5 kilometres of the project" (para. 8) [S23].
  - The plant's 12 MW is a factual observation and sits below the 1P2007 PGF-Large DC trigger of 12.5 MW. **Whether 1P2007 treats it as a PGF use, and the site's land use district, are UNVERIFIED.**

## 5. Rocky View County and Airdrie

### Rocky View County (bounds Calgary to the north, east and west)

- **Data-centre district.** Bylaw **C-8648-2025** amends LUB C-8000-2020 "to add a new land use district (Special, Data Centre District)". Its stated purpose is "to provide for the development of data centre facilities and uses complementary to the principal Data Centre use" (public hearing 17 June 2025) [S24]. The County's LUB amendment list shows **C-8766-2026, approved 21 July 2026: "Various amendments to the S-DAT District and Data Centre Campus"**. The LUB table of contents places S-DAT in Part 6 (p. 103) and an S-DAT-A overlay in Part 7 [S25]. An "SDAT-B" overlay is named in the Kineticor notice [S27]. **The S-DAT district text (uses, setbacks, height) could not be read** because the online copies are truncated (GAP).
- **Beacon AI Hub ASP, Bylaw C-8638-2025, approved 17 June 2025** [S26, S28]. The site is about 900 acres, "approximately 3.2 kilometres (2 miles) east of the city of Calgary" (Sec. 11 and parts of Sec. 14-23-28-W4M). The lands were redesignated to S-DAT (Bylaw C-8645-2025). Key policies:
  - **Definition (glossary):** "A large-scale facility designed to house extensive computer systems and associated components", including ancillary offices, training, maintenance, power and security buildings.
  - **Setback:** 30 m from adjacent property lines, with fencing and/or landscaping (8.3, 9.10).
  - **Noise:** compliance with AUC Rule 012 and Bylaw C-8067-2020. A noise impact assessment is required at the DP stage. Generators should be in sound-attenuated enclosures (8.10–8.13, 20.2).
  - **Lighting:** dark-sky principles and a lighting plan (8.14–8.16).
  - **Water:** reuse is encouraged. Water-based cooling designs and the water source are assessed at the DP stage. A piped water connection is required. No potable water for non-residential irrigation, except for 2 years during establishment (8.17–8.19, 15.x).
  - **Power:** minimize grid impact and use renewables where possible (8.20–8.22). Studies are required for utility-scale generation (15.34).
  - **Equipment:** cooling, ventilation and power equipment "fully enclosed where mechanically feasible", otherwise screened (21.21–21.22).
  - **Height:** no data-centre-specific limit; the district applies (21.8).
  - **Landscaping:** tree minimums of 63 mm calliper (deciduous) and 2.5 m (coniferous) (9.4, 9.7, 21.27).
  - *(Section numbers come from the fetch tool's reading of the ASP PDF. Spot-check them against the PDF.)*
- **Beacon power plant at AUC.** Indus Power Generation LP filed on 29 Dec 2025 for "a **1,494 MWe** natural gas-fired power plant" that "will supply ~**1,200 MWe** to co-located, on-site data halls". The plant would have 100 × 4.6 MWe Jenbacher J624 reciprocating engines (460 MWe) and 12 × 61.2 MWe Siemens SGT-800 turbines with HRSGs feeding four 75 MWe steam turbines (1,034 MWe). It "will eventually be connected to the transmission system" [S29]. AUC Proceeding **30524** [S28]. On 10 Feb 2026 Rocky View Council directed staff to take part formally, citing resident concerns about "noise, emissions, water availability and servicing, and the protection of agricultural land" [S30]. **No AUC decision was found (status UNVERIFIED).** The ASP says nothing about using the waste heat (heat recovery not found in the policies read).
- **Kineticor Data Centre Campus ASP (C-8671-2025) and redesignation to SDAT-B (C-8672-2025) were refused on 9 Sept 2025.** The site was "approximately 448 hectares (± 1,107 acres)" in the northeast. It "could accommodate multiple data centres and possibly on-site natural gas power generation". The decision was "mainly based on concerns about the proposed location and its potential effect on local agricultural producers". AESO had "dedicated an electricity allocation to a proposed data centre in Phase 1" [S31]. The County's follow-up release says it has "dedicated Data Centre Land Use Bylaw requirements and a streamlined application review process", and cites eStruxture Cal-2 and Cal-3 in east Balzac [S32].
- **eStruxture CAL-2, 149 High Plains Place, Rocky View County.** AUC Decision **27614-D01-2022** (4 Oct 2022) approved a **12 MW** backup diesel plant: 3 × 3 MW KOHLER KD3250 plus 1 × 3 MW MTU. It is not connected to the AIES and has no residences within 1.5 km. Rule 012 compliance was accepted (para. 6) [S33].
- **Emerging Sector Strategy – Technology & Energy.** Council directed the work in April 2025 and approved the Terms of Reference in November 2025. A research report, "ESS – Data Centre, Technology, Impacts and Municipal Policy Considerations", is out. Open houses run 29 Sept to 15 Oct 2026. The Options Report goes to Council in **July 2027** [S34].
- **Moratorium (UNVERIFIED).** News reports (Calgary Journal, 28 July 2026) say Council voted 5–2 for a "temporary moratorium" on new redesignations for data centres until the strategy and framework are done. **No official Rocky View minute or release was found.** The County's engagement page "does not mention any pause" [S34].

### City of Airdrie

- **Land Use Bylaw B-01/2016, consolidated 8 Sept 2026**, defines **"Data Centre"**: "The industrial use of a building, dedicated space within a building, or a group of buildings to house computer systems and associated components, such as telecommunications and storage systems." [S35]
- **The districts listing it (for example the IB-2 and IB-3 industrial business districts) could not be read** because the online text is truncated (GAP, UNVERIFIED). No Airdrie data-centre policy or ASP was found.

### Also adjacent (found incidentally): City of Chestermere

- **Bylaw 014-25, "Data Centre Land Use Bylaw Amendment" (to LUB 020-24).** It adds "Data Centre Campus" as a **permitted** use in BP-LI (10.15.2). It sets height at "20.0 m for a Data Centre Campus" (10.14.4) and coverage at "60% of the total lot area or as supported by a stormwater management plan". ## 6. Alberta level: AESO and Government of Alberta (2024–2026)

- **AESO interim limit (4 June 2025):** "1,200 MW interim limit"; "Large load connection requests are outpacing available generation in Alberta" [S37].
- **GoA data centre grid page:** the 1,200 MW is "now fully taken". About **19,565 MW** of data centre load has been requested (AESO list, 30 July 2026), against a provincial record peak of about 12,800 MW. Projects that pair load with "dedicated new generation or storage" "go to the front of the connection line". A **Data Centre Regulation came into force in June 2026**. The 2024 self-supply-with-export rules allow on-site generation with export [S38].
- **Utilities Statutes Amendment Act, 2025 (Bill 8), royal assent 11 Dec 2025:** "encourage data centres to bring their own generation"; "data centres (not Alberta ratepayers) will pay for any necessary transmission upgrades" [S39].
- **AESO Phase 2A, Bring-Your-Own-Generation (BYOG):** the working group launched on 5 Nov 2025 and the proposed process was posted on 26 June 2026. The 8 Sept 2026 update says the AESO is "still working to finalize the process", with "application intake to begin this fall" [S40].
- **Data centre levy:** in consultation, with options of a levy on computing equipment value, on electricity consumed, or "compute-in-kind" credits. No rates have been set (page last updated 7 July 2025) [S41].

Calgary's distinctive lever is the **12.5 MW Direct Control trigger for on-site generation**, which Edmonton (per file 18) does not appear to have.
