## 1. What the project is

**Who.** Jenna Bruggeman (J.B.) is a 3rd-year M.Arch student at UBC.

**The thesis.** GP1 thesis with Matthew Soules; chair Annalisa Meyboom. It asks how architecture might connect the physical capacities of data centres with human needs: when and how they might contribute to urban life beyond digital services.

**The tool.** "Beyond Data" is ARCH 540 Assignment 2 and is built alongside the thesis. For any parcel in Vancouver or Edmonton, and a data centre of about 0.1–40 MW, it shows:
- what could fit on the parcel;
- what it could exchange with its surroundings in both directions: heat, power, water, space, materials and sound;
- what each exchange would cost and who it would affect.

**Key dates.** Mid review 23 Oct 2026. User sessions by 6 Nov. Final package 10 Dec.

## 3. How the tool reasons (decided 12–14 Oct)

**No score, no ranking, no verdict on a match.**
- Each condition is rated: met · met with a change · not met · not applicable or declined · cannot judge · not judged.
- Options appear side by side in no order. Each has a trade-off table by party, with every row in its own unit and nothing summed. Three options by default.

**Constraints**
- Only physics is a hard constraint.
- Every other condition says what would move it: a different option · money · time and process · policy · city-scale infrastructure.
- A user's limits are respected but never silent. The "If you moved a limit" panel shows what relaxing a limit would open up, including rules from Fairfax, Loudoun, Frankfurt, Germany (EnEfG), the EU and Calgary.

**Parties.** Operator, host, receiver, utility, city, neighbours, the public passing by, ratepayers, workers, future residents, the climate, and First Nations as rights holders. The tool describes the rights and processes that apply to First Nations and never speaks for them.

**Headings.** Conditions sit under 11 headings: technical, spatial, temporal, economic, organisational, regulatory, security, social, environmental, experiential, and "what if we added".

**Starting step.** New or existing building, then whole or part, then (for part of an existing building) which floor. Special cases: a modular unit, a site beside existing infrastructure, or an expansion.

**Confidence rings.** 100 arithmetic · 90 regulation read · 80 government data · 70 peer-reviewed and transferred · 60 derived · 50 operator claim · 40 proxy · 20 judged · 0 gap. A condition can be no surer than its weakest figure.

## 4. Added 14 Oct, from a critique of J.B.'s learning-series manuscript

The manuscript *Beyond Data: a narrated introduction*, about 25,000 words, is a learning aid. Most of its sources are models: Montreal (Shafiee Roudbari 2025), Lausanne (Ravi 2026), global wastewater (Wang 2026) and EV coordination (Zhang 2025). Quincy (EPA) is a built system. None of those papers has been read yet, so their figures are unverified. Two are inconsistent within the manuscript itself:
- The Montreal 74% is described as one building's demand in chapter 2 and the campus's in chapter 17.
- Lausanne's solar self-consumption is "up to 90%" in chapter 4 and "raised by up to 28%" in chapter 10. (Atlas chat, 15 Oct: the 28 % is an increase in buffering potential for variable renewables; the 90 % appears nowhere.)

The register's calibration cases are built systems and are the stronger evidence: Odense, Mäntsälä, Stockholm, Helsinki/Telia, Tallaght, PA10 and Seattle Westin (sources/13).

What the critique added to the register (script: add_exchange.py):

**Mechanism.** Each exchange names how it happens, and the diagram draws each one differently:
- physical transfer: solid line
- coordination through the grid: dotted
- shared space: long dash
- shared service: dash-dot

Coordination through the grid (shifting computing, smart charging) is a service to the utility, not something the building delivers to its neighbours.

**Evidence type.** Shown alongside the confidence ring: operating · measured · operator claim · modelled · proposed · speculative.

**Firmness.** Each exchange is existing, committed, planned, proposed or possible. Only existing and committed exchanges count as delivered.

**Next actions.** All 100 conditions carry one for when the tool cannot judge them, e.g. route survey, hourly data, cost model, find a partner.

**Gates first.** If the grid connection or the zoning use is blocked, the tool says so before weighing any exchange. Downtown Vancouver has no new feeder positions until the new substation (July 2032).

**Four futures.** Each option is run as planned, with the tenant changing its cooling, with the tenant leaving, and with the receiver leaving.

**New quantities:**
- receivers within reach
- other sources already serving the same receiver
- what the receiver uses now
- an hour-by-hour match
- who controls the computing
- how firm the exchange is
- futures

**Carbon.** Carbon is judged against the source the heat would displace, so it can be zero or negative. The City says the NEU is already 70% renewable, mostly sewage heat. Blatchford in Edmonton runs on geo-exchange and sewer heat.

**Hot water for taps.** Must be stored at 60 °C or more, and recirculated at 49 °C or more anywhere in the loop (VBBL 2025 Book II, 2.6.1.12.(1) and 2.6.1.1.(3)). Data centre heat at 25–35 °C can therefore only preheat tap water. The Alberta text still needs confirming.

**Results from elsewhere.** These sit under "If you moved a limit", labelled by evidence type, and are never used as a parcel's own numbers.

## 6. The tool page (build round 1, published 14 Oct)

**Data.** Both cities are tiled from open data:
- Vancouver: parcels, 2009 footprints, zoning, floodplain, and every civic address.
- Edmonton: parcel points, roofline estimates, zoning, and account addresses.

**Flow.** City → parcel or address search → site card → "What is being built?"
- The site card shows the zone, whether a data centre is an allowed use, the lot, values, buildings and sources.
- Downtown plan areas and Edmonton special-area zones show as "not yet reviewed" rather than a guess.

**LiDAR.** Building heights from the City of Vancouver LiDAR 2022 are being merged with the 2009 footprints, flagging buildings that are gone or new. 129 of 148 tiles are processed; J.B. finishes the downloads at home.

**Still to build:**

- Round 3: three options with trade-off tables.
- Round 4: what-ifs and limits.
- Round 5: the dark 3D view, Guide links, then testing.
