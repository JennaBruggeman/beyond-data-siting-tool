# Calibration of the tool against built cases — 13 October 2026

What this is: the tool's formulas run on the published inputs of operating data-centre heat-recovery systems, compared with the published outcomes, and the corrections that followed. Inputs and quotes are in `sources/13_calibration_cases.md` (four retrievals, verbatim, with their access notes); the arithmetic is `calibrate.py`; the corrections are in `formula_register.json` (meta.calibration_note) and the tool. Status labels follow the hunt: METERED · REPORTED (utility or operator) · OPERATOR CLAIM · MODELLED.

## 1. Which cases have numbers worth calibrating on

Of twenty-odd cases found, eight publish enough to test a formula. Odense (Meta → Fjernvarme Fyn) is the only one with a utility-settled year of heat output and heat-pump electricity attributed to the data centre alone (2020: 230 TJ from 14,020 MWh, COP 4.5). Telia Helsinki publishes a one-day snapshot with COP 3.9, PUE 1.152 and the only ERF on the ISO basis (0.75) — an operator claim. Mäntsälä (Yandex → Nivos) gives delivered heat against the data centre's power demand (20 GWh in 2018 from a ~15 MW facility). Heerlen (APG → Mijnwater) gives extracted heat against a 520 kW facility. Tallaght (AWS) gives a first metered year (3,770 MWh from a 3 MW heat pump). TELUS Garden, Vancouver, gives regulator-reviewed design figures (1,400 kW base-load offer against a 5,194 MWh heating demand, 94 % covered). Université Laval gives a campus-loop case with a reported COP (~7 to 40 °C). Equinix PA10, ReUseHeat Braunschweig and Westville add capital costs and a design COP. No case publishes the IT load *and* the delivered heat *and* the price together; no Canadian case publishes a metered annual figure.

## 2. What the comparison showed

**COP.** The first attempt used a simplified Lorenz form (sink supply ÷ (sink supply − source out), η 0.50–0.60). Against Odense (27→15 °C source, 40→75 °C network) it gives 3.0–3.6 where the utility settled 4.5 in 2020 and 4.2 all-in over 2021–22. The full Lorenz form with log-mean temperatures on both sides and η 0.50 gives 4.56 — within 1 % of the realised 4.5. Solving η from each case's realised COP: Odense 0.49 and 0.46, Telia 0.39, Laval 0.53; the two design/simulated values (ReUseHeat 3.6, Bahnhof 3.7) imply 0.37 and 0.43. **Correction: full Lorenz, η 0.40–0.50, capped at 8** (the highest realised value among the cases is Laval's ~7). The simplified form is retired. Temperatures now enter as supply/return on the sink and in/out on the source, with the source cooled 10–12 K (Odense 12, Telia 12, ReUseHeat 7).

**Delivered heat is set by the receiver, not the source.** In every built case with a large data centre the network took a small fraction of what the data centre could give: Mäntsälä 15–23 % of nameplate-continuous output, Westville 1 % of a 35 MW facility, TELUS Garden 40 % of the 1.4 MW offer. Only Heerlen — a 520 kW facility feeding a whole low-temperature network — ran source-limited (54 %). The tool computed heat *available* and sized opportunities from it as if it were delivered. **Correction: `heat_delivered` is renamed "heat available after heat pump"; a new quantity `heat_matched` = min(available, receiver demand) is the match; homes-heated is labelled a sizing, not a delivery.** For 111 E 5th this is decisive: the data centre could supply 57–361 GWh a year and the NEU, on a proxy, needs 44–59.

**Utilisation.** Telia's snapshot (4.11 MW of waste heat at ERF 0.75 and PUE 1.152) implies 4.8 MW actual against 24 MW IT nameplate — 0.20, below the tool's 0.35 floor, on a partly built-out hall. **Correction: utilisation band 0.20–0.60** (LBNL's 0.21 server-utilisation floor now coincides with a measured facility figure). This widens every downstream range; that is the honest consequence of a one-day measurement, and narrows only with a metered load.

**Measurement boundary.** Published "heat delivered" figures use two different boundaries and the tool had conflated them. ISO/IEC 30134-6 and EU Regulation 2024/1364 count only heat crossing the data-centre boundary before any heat pump (E_reuse, measured by flow × ΔT at the handoff); heat-pump uplift and electricity are excluded. Utilities meter and bill on the network side after their heat pump (Stockholm Exergi owns the meter; Fjernvarme Fyn reports DH output ≈ 1.3 × the DC-side heat; Danish tax law meters at the heat-pump outlet and deducts 3 × electricity). **Correction: a new quantity `heat_captured` (E_reuse) sits between heat rejected and heat available; ERF is redefined as E_reuse ÷ E_DC (it equals the captured share, 0.5–0.8 for air/glycol, 0.9–1.0 for a condenser loop), and the first attempt's ERF (delivered ÷ IT energy, which exceeded 1) is withdrawn.** The NEU by-law's "heat energy returned" (credited at 50 % of the energy rate) is undefined by any standard; the two local figures that matter for a Vancouver match are E_reuse at the ETS and the heat the NEU actually accepts.

**Who pays.** In five of the seven cases with ownership on record the data centre gives the heat free and the utility owns and pays for the heat pump (Odense: Fjernvarme Fyn pays the whole 215 M DKK; Tallaght: SDCC-owned Heat Works, AWS heat free; Equinix PA10: free for 15 years; Markham: "does not seek income"; TELUS Garden: FAES owns the system, "free waste heat supply"). Stockholm and Mäntsälä are the supplier-owned, paid model. **Correction: the tool now states both models; under model A the operator floor is zero and the economic test is whether the utility's all-in cost beats its alternative, under model B the floor must be paid.** The register's "operator floor price" gap is therefore less important than it looked — the more common question is the utility's, and the tool can answer it.

**Full-load hours, capital, price.** Ramp-up years run 1,300–2,700 full-load hours (Tallaght year 1, Odense 2020); mature plants 4,400–7,100 (Odense 2024, Mäntsälä, Inspirationskatalog design 5,000–7,000, STACK Oslo claim 7,143). The tool's assumed 4,000–6,000 holds for a mature year and overstates the first ones. Capital: 0.66 M€/MW all-in at Odense's 44 MW scale, 0.86 at Equinix PA10 including 2.6 km of network, 1.53 at ReUseHeat's 0.3 MW; the register's DEA 0.95–1.13 sits inside. Price: Stockholm's €190k per MW·yr at 5,000 h is €38/MWh ≈ C$57, the same as the register's 400 SEK figure; TELUS Garden's customer rate ($105–126/MWh, BC Hydro residential + 5–10 %) is the first Vancouver price for a recovered-heat service, all-in.

## 3. Effect on the three test runs

| | first attempt (12 Oct) | calibrated (13 Oct) | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 555 W Hastings, IT actual | 1.3–2.2 MW | 0.7–2.2 MW | utilisation floor 0.20 |
| 555, COP | 3.1–4.6 (to a 70/35 network) | 6.0–8 (to the host's 45/35 loop) | full Lorenz; the steam network is now outside the run (steam-raising heat pumps are outside the calibrated η and the steam temperature is not in the register) |
| 555, heat available / matched | 10–37 GWh/yr (called "delivered") | 5–30 available; 4.9 matched (host demand) | receiver cap |
| 555, floor / payback | $39–76; 4.9 yr to never | $31–58; 4–24 yr | higher COP at the low lift |
| 111 E 5th, matched | — | 44–59 of 57–361 GWh available | receiver cap; the NEU cannot take the rest |
| Kyndryl, verdict | doesn't work | doesn't work | Alberta gas ceiling unchanged |

The headline verdicts did not move; the numbers behind them did, and now carry a source for every step that was previously an assumption.

## 3b. The cases inside the tool (added 13 Oct, evening)

The seven cases now sit in the tool as a second site group beside the three test sites, run through the same engine from their published inputs, with the published outcome beside each computed range. Nineteen published figures are compared. Twelve fall inside the tool's range; three sit within 1 % of its edge (Telia's COP 3.9 against 3.96–5.1 and its IT load 4.76 MW against 4.8–14.4, both from a one-day snapshot; Laval's 1.15 GWh against 1.17–1.27); two sit 6–7 % outside (TELUS Garden's 94 % coverage, because the design peaks on steam, and its $3.2 M recovery option against the tool's $2.3–3.0 M); and two are the ramp-year full-load hours (Odense 2020 at 2,662 h, Tallaght year 1 at 1,257 h) against the tool's mature-year 4,000–6,000, which the tool now states as a known limit rather than a fit. Change η, the utilisation band or the capture share in the constants and the fit rows re-run, so the calibration stays live rather than being a report about a past state of the tool.

## 4. What is still uncalibrated

The capture share for air/glycol loops (0.5–0.8, judged) has no built-case check — no case publishes E_reuse and heat rejected together; Telia's 0.75 ERF is the nearest, for a liquid loop. The electricity price ($80–120/MWh, assumed) and the exchange rate remain assumptions. Receiver demand for a network (NEU, Creative Energy) is still a proxy. No case gives a Canadian metered year; TELUS Garden's actual-load reporting was rescinded by the regulator in 2015, so the one Vancouver precedent has design figures only. Nothing here tests the non-heat matches (power, water, space, materials) — the built record is almost entirely heat to district networks.
