Drafting conventions throughout: three line weights (structure, flows, notes), numbered tags instead of labels on the drawing; a box gets its number as a badge with no leader; every other leader runs only orthogonal or at 45°, and tags line up in columns or rows in empty space; tags are numbered in reading order; a key under each figure with the node names, and the selected node in red. Why each node is here
  • Heat rejected MWh/yr
    Almost all the electricity a data centre uses ends up as heat; this is the total that has to go somewhere.
  • Share of heat captured in liquid —
    Heat caught in liquid at the chip is warmer and easier to pass on than heat in air; the share sets how much is usable.
  • Heat temperature by cooling type °C
    The temperature decides who can use the heat directly, and how hard a heat pump has to work.
  • Heat captured at the boundary (E_reuse) MWh/yr
    The heat that crosses the data centre boundary into a reuse system: the quantity the international standard (ISO/IEC 30134-6) asks to be measured.
  • Energy reuse factor —
    Captured heat as a share of the energy used: the standard's headline ratio, and how the built cases are compared.
  • Heat-pump COP (Lorenz) —
    How much heat a heat pump gives per unit of electricity at these temperatures.
  • Heat-pump electricity MWh/yr
    Lifting the heat to network temperature costs electricity; counting it keeps the gain honest.
  • Heat available after heat pump MWh/yr
    What reaches the network after the heat pump: the figure the neighbours can actually use.
  • Heat carried in water kWh
    Heat carried per volume of water, which sizes the pipes and the flow.
  • Free-cooling hours h/yr
    Hours when outside air alone can cool the data centre; they change how much heat is worth capturing.

Reading the side panel

  • 1Family and kind
    Which family the item belongs to (its icon) and whether it is an input, a quantity or a condition.
  • 2Name and unit
    Each figure stays in its own unit; nothing is converted to a common score.
  • 3Confidence ring
    How far the tool can trust the figure: full is arithmetic, three-quarters a regulation or government data, half an operator's claim.
  • 4Diagram
    Where the figure sits in the mechanism it belongs to.
  • 5Formula
    How it is worked out, from which other figures.
  • 6Sources and their roles
    Which source gives which term, and how sure that placement is.
  • 7Conditions it feeds
    Which conditions are judged on it, and what would move them.
