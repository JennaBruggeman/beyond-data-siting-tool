# Site decisions and findings, 22 Oct 2026

Decided with J.B. Every figure has its source; estimates are marked as such.

## The two sites (kept)

- **Vancouver: 275 1st Ave E.** The flagship case.
- **Edmonton: 8803 Stadium Rd NW.** Kept over 12503 66 St NW because it's civic, not an industrial park: people score 0.85; 0.2 % industrial land within 1 km; about 8,700 people within 800 m; 35 heat users in reach (about 54,700 MWh/yr counted).

## Vancouver: the NEU case

- **The site is inside the NEU service area.**
  - Confirmed on the Council report's Appendix E map ([RTS 18251, Oct 2025](https://council.vancouver.ca/20251112/documents/spec1d.pdf)).
- **The NEU is looking for data-centre heat.**
  - The report lists "commercial cooling, and data centres" as local heat sources.
  - Its expert panel urges waste heat from "planned data centers".
  - The 2024 Decarbonization Roadmap shortlists "local waste heat from data centres/industry" ([IDEA 2026 application](https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/DISTRICTENERGY/998638d1-8c22-4b53-960c-286248642360/UploadedImages/Award_Photos/SOYA2026/ENG-NEU-IDEA_SOYA_2026_Application.pdf)).
- **The gap the data centre could fill:**
  - The NEU supplies about 63 GWh/yr (2025), forecast to reach about 157 GWh by 2035.
  - Within that, "Expanded Low-carbon Energy (Sources to be Determined)" grows to about 60 GWh by 2035 (read off Figure 5, approximate).
  - The data centre could offer 33–60 GWh/yr (tool, liquid cooling + heat pump).
- **The NEU's 2025 sources** (Figure 4):

  | Source | Share |
  |---|---|
  | Sewer heat | 60 % |
  | Natural gas | 29 % |
  | RNG | 10 % |
  | Customer waste heat | 1 % |

  Capacity is 39 MW: 29 MW of gas boilers and 9.8 MW of sewage heat. The goal is 100 % renewable by 2030.
- **The credit:** heat returned through a building's connection is credited at $65.172 × 50 % = **$32.59/MWh** ([By-law 9552, Sched. C Pt 4, s. 8.3](https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/9552c.PDF)).
  - **Open:** whether a source this large would use this credit or a separate agreement.
- **Temperature:** the network runs at a minimum of 65 °C ([NEU Connectivity Guidelines, May 2026, s. 6.3](https://guidelines.vancouver.ca/guidelines-neighbourhood-energy-utility-connectivity-requirements.pdf)). A heat pump is needed (liquid cooling gives 50–60 °C).
- **The pipe, inferred** (not confirmed): a main most likely runs along **E 1st Ave**, about 100–150 m from the lot.
  - Emily Carr (520 E 1st Ave, about 360 m) is connected: confirmed ([ECUAD](https://guides.ecuad.ca/sustainability/buildingandfacilities)).
  - 375 E 1st Ave (2020, 10,200 m², CD-1 (402), the same zone as Emily Carr, about 125 m) is likely connected, because new buildings over 2,000 m² in the area must connect ([Council, June 2012](https://council.vancouver.ca/20120612/documents/a4.pdf)).
  - Draw it as a dashed "assumed main". Compare: the tool's best direct pipe was 1,515 m.
- **EV:** 119 Level 2 and 9 fast ports within 1 km (NRCan); 13,500 households, 59 % renters (Census 2021). The opportunity is a **fast-charging hub on the data centre's grid connection**, not more Level 2.

## Edmonton: designing with the rules

- **Develop option B:** a smaller, contained data centre, argued as Minor Industrial under the current IM zoning, and sized to the neighbours' heat.
- **The policy proposal:** a conditional, Direct Control-style zone. It permits the data centre only if it shares heat, meets a night noise limit, includes the public refuge (now ruled out: "Community Service" isn't listed in IM), and keeps hazards on site.
  - **Not:** a rezoning to IH to fit a bigger building.
  - DC zones under Bylaw 20001 are still to verify.
- **EV:** a real gap (2 chargers within 1.5 km).
- **Heat:** worth about $13/MWh. Downtown District Energy (planned phases) is about 915 m away, as the long-term route.

## Ideas tested and set aside

| Idea | Why it's set aside |
|---|---|
| North Saskatchewan River loop | Valley plan (Bylaw 7188) bans utility corridors except crossings; +3 °C return limit; Water Act licence; frazil ice; no river temperature data; no data-centre river precedent. It also competes with selling heat to neighbours. |
| Borefield as the main cooling | About 2 ha per MW (register): 30+ ha needed against lots of under 1 ha. Epic Systems needed 6,100 wells for 3.5 MW. |
| Deep geothermal, SMRs, the Rossdale site | Speculative or blocked. Rossdale is in the River Valley ARP and the floodplain. |
| Copilot notes | Several errors. Air exhaust is 25–35 °C, not 50 °C. Floor loads are unsourced. Fuel rules come from CSA C282 and the fire codes, not NFPA 110 directly. Keep only the program items (meet-me room, gas suppression, VESDA, staging), to be verified. |

## Kept as speculative (one move)

- **A seasonal ground store** for neighbours. It fixes the measured timing gap: neighbours' demand swings about 5× between months while the data centre runs steadily. Strongest in Vancouver. Studies only, cost unknown.
