## Findings and proposed changes (for the gate)

**1. Lot area larger than its frame (98 Vancouver lots, 24 Edmonton).** Irregularity above 1 is not possible for one polygon. In Vancouver the tool's lot area counts every part of a tax coordinate, but the frame and the edges are of one part (e.g. 1010 Raymur Ave 21960106: 4,671 m² on a 38.5 × 8.0 m frame, irregularity 15.1). Edmonton's 24 are slivers under 15 m² (rounding). This does not match the job sheet's definitions; **question to Cowork** (checks/to_cowork.md). Until answered they are typed as the rules give (none falls under 'irregular').

**2. Corner.** By edge count (job sheet) half the lots are corners (Vancouver 393 of 699, Edmonton 3,125 of 5,232), because one street drawn as several polygon segments counts more than once. By angle (street lines at least 45° apart): Vancouver 332, Edmonton 2,186. Proposed: corner by angle. Counts in table A above. Corner is also tested second, so it absorbs many lots that are small, large or deep; a flag column may serve the specs better than a type (question).

**3. Aspect.** No natural break at 2.5: Vancouver's largest bin is 2.0–2.5 (128 lots), and 339 1st Ave E is 2.43 with a 61 m depth limit on a 112 m lot. With the depth limit the drawn building is what matters. Proposed: narrow_deep at aspect ≥ 2.0. Wide_shallow ≤ 0.8 holds few lots; keep.

**4. Small, large, irregular.** Buildable area has no break near 853 or 6,000 m² (smooth spread); keep both (853 is the smallest typology, a real limit). Irregularity: 0.75 sits in a smooth tail; keep.

**5. Buildable area.** The job sheet says 'inside the yard lines (the tool's buildable polygon)'; these differ on I-2/I-3 lots where the 61 m depth limit applies (275 1st Ave E: 6,090 vs 3,743 m², large vs regular). The tables use the yard lines; table B uses the limit. Question to Cowork.
