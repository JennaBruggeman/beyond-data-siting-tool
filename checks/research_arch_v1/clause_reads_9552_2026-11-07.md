# By-law 9552 (Energy Utility System, Vancouver NEU): what a connected new building must take — clause read, 7 Nov 2026 (Code)

Source: City of Vancouver Energy Utility System By-law No. 9552, "Consolidated for convenience only to December 9, 2025" (local copy: Claude Link beyond-data-sources/public/neu9552/full.pdf, saved 25 Oct 2026 from https://bylaws.vancouver.ca/9552c.PDF; same file as Downloads/9552c.PDF). Register: van9552. Quotes are verbatim. "?" = not settled.

## Who must connect

- **s. 2.1:** "Each owner in the service area of: (a) a new building proposed for construction … must make use of the energy utility system in accordance with the terms and conditions of this By-law, unless the City Engineer is of the opinion that providing the service to a building is not practical or economical." (b) covers existing buildings with alterations over the greater of $95,000 or 100 % of assessed value.
- 339 1st Ave E is inside the service area (Schedule A trace in the tool, 182 m in, ±45 m). A new data-centre building there must connect unless the City Engineer says otherwise.

## What it must take

- **"heat energy"** (s. 1.2): "heat distributed or delivered by water including space heating, domestic hot water, and heat for ventilation make-up air".
- **s. 3.1:** the system is to "supply the entire heat energy demand for each designated building".
- **s. 5.2:** "A building mechanical system must utilize the energy utility system for all the space heating and domestic hot water requirements for a designated building, and must not incorporate any heat production equipment including boilers, furnaces, hot water heaters or make-up air heaters, air-source or ground-source heat pumps, except that:"
- **s. 5.3(b):** "the energy utility system must provide the heat energy requirements for all domestic hot water and space heating for the designated building supplied from the energy transfer station within the designated building".

## Exemptions and supplements (s. 5.2)

- **(a)** a new or altered building may, "for the purpose of supplementing the heat energy provided by the energy utility system", incorporate: (i) a solar system to generate heat energy; (ii) distributed water-to-air heat pump systems "where the source of heat is the energy utility system or waste heat recovered inside the building"; (iii) water-source VRF systems on the same terms; (iv) electric resistance heating in stairwells and isolated below-grade bike lockers and storage rooms; **(v) "equipment to recover waste heat energy from the refrigeration or cooling system of the building"**.
- **(b)** alterations may keep otherwise prohibited components where the Chief Building Official (and City Engineer) permits.
- **(c)** a new building, or an altered non-residential building, "with a peak heat energy demand intensity exceeding 125 watts per square meter" may add on-site heat production equipment to reduce the peak load on the utility, but must "(i) prioritize the energy utility system as the building's primary heating source", "(ii) only activate the on-site heat production equipment when the building's total heat energy demand intensity … exceeds 125 watts per square meter", and "(iii) … only generate the portion of the building's peak heat energy demand above 125 watts per square meter". s. 4.7: the City may review such a design.

## Heat sent back

- **s. 8.3:** the owner pays "the charge set out in Part 3 of Schedule C less the credit set out in Part 4 of Schedule C".
- **Schedule C Part 4:** "Credit for heat energy returned to energy transfer station — $65.172 per each MW hour multiplied by 50%" (= $32.59/MWh, our arithmetic). Energy charge (Part 3): $65.172/MWh Standard Green Heat. Connection levy (Part 1, from 1 Jan 2026): $123,873 per energy transfer station + $146 per kW of approved peak heat energy demand. Capacity levy, non-residential (Class 3): $10.466 per kW of peak heat energy demand per month.

## Reading for the heat card (our reading, ?)

- A connected data centre must take **all its space heating, domestic hot water and make-up air heating** from the NEU. Its own server waste heat may only **supplement** that, through the equipment listed in 5.2(a): heat recovery from its cooling system (v), or water-to-air / water-source VRF heat pumps fed by waste heat recovered inside the building (ii, iii). It may not replace the NEU as the primary source.
- Sending surplus heat to the network: the by-law prices heat "returned to [the] energy transfer station" at 50 % of the energy charge, but sets no other terms for supplying the network (whether the City must accept it, temperatures, volumes): not in this by-law (?). The NEU Connectivity Guidelines (register neuConnect, May 2026 Rev. 1) are the place to read those.
- A data centre's own heat demand is small next to its waste heat, so the obligation mostly matters for the levies: connection and capacity levies are owed on the approved peak heat demand even if recovered heat covers much of it (our reading, ?).
- Whether the City Engineer would find service "not practical or economical" for a data centre (s. 2.1) is a question for the City (?).
