## 1. What the project is

A tool (ARCH 540 AI-studio Assignment 2, built alongside the GP1 thesis with Matthew Soules) that tests whether a data centre on any parcel in Vancouver or Edmonton could share heat, power, water, space, materials or sound with its neighbours. A "match" is one output or capacity plus one receiver. The tool scores an open list of conditions pass / partial / fail under ten headings (technical, spatial, temporal, economic, organizational, regulatory, security, social, environmental, experiential), non-compensatory — burdens are recorded beside benefits, never netted — with "No" a valid answer, ranges rather than points, and operator figures labelled as claims. Mid review 23 Oct; a user needed by 6 Nov; three user sessions; final package 10 Dec.

Standing rules: nothing made up or assumed, verify against sources and mark anything unverified; no popular sources; label operator claims; don't circumvent blocked sites; Canadian spelling; J.B. writes the prose (suggested wording goes in red for her to accept); plain professional visual style (soft red #CC746C, greys, Open Sans Light; nothing that reads as AI-made); on security: J.B. never set a 'no security details' rule (corrected 11 Oct); the working line is narrower — nothing that works as a guide to defeating security (blind spots, coverage gaps, entry routes, equipment models, staffing times); surveillance as experienced from public space is in scope. Sound is one small component of many and must not dominate the experiential heading.

Order J.B. set: map the workflows → interactive diagram → exhaustive formula search and full process definition → then, and only then, build the tool. The formula search is now substantially complete (this file). The diagram rebuild on the four-node grammar is the next step; the tool build has not started.

## 2. The register (the authoritative artefact)

`formula_register.json` is the single source of truth; `formula_register.md` is generated from it by `make_register_md.py`. Edit the JSON, run the script.

- 85 quantities, each with id, formula, unit, inputs, coefficients, sources, own confidence, status and process stage (size → characterise → prospect → test → decide); upstream confidence = minimum over inputs.
- 66 conditions under the ten headings (from GP1, 8 Oct 2026), each tied to the quantities that test it.
- 14 opportunities, each tied to the quantities that size it (homes heated, EV stalls, greenhouse area, aquaculture tonnes, refuge places, etc.).
- Confidence scale: 100 arithmetic · 90 regulation/tariff/standard read · 80 government data · 70 peer-reviewed coefficient transferred · 60 derived · 50 operator/industry claim · 40 proxy · 20 judged · 0 gap. Current spread: 9 at 100, 18 at 90, 14 at 80, 25 at 70, 9 at 60, 5 at 50, 4 at 40, 1 at 20, 0 gaps.
- Four-node grammar for the diagram: source (confidence-rated) → quantity (formula, unit, range) → condition → verdict (works / works with a change / doesn't; blocker named).

Supporting files: `sources/01–11_*.md` (raw hunt reports by theme), `verification_report.md` (258 items re-verified, 10 mismatches corrected), `retrieval_guide.md` (what J.B. fetches, with status), `data/` (every primary file she has retrieved plus derived JSON extracts), `workflow-map.html` (the v3 diagram, to be rebuilt).

## 3. Headline findings worth knowing

Sizing and the claims-versus-actual distinction
- RVO 4.38 MWh/m²·yr (≈500 W/m²) for sizing a data centre from the outside; LBNL 600 W/server, idle 36%, colocation utilisation 21→35%.
- Vancouver's Annual Energy and Carbon Report (reporting year 2025) gives measured whole-building energy for four sites. Harbour Centre (555 W Hastings, 73,225 m²) uses 37.4 GWh/yr, 32.5 GWh non-heat ≈ 3.7 MW average for the whole tower; operator nameplate claims for its tenants add to ≥3.7 MW, so they cannot all run flat out. This is the first measured ceiling on actual load. The host building's own heat demand is only 15–22% of its non-heat energy, so a host can absorb at most about a sixth of what its data centre rejects.
- Vancouver building permits (public record) name the plant at each site: rooftop condensers (1050 W Pender, 175 W Cordova), rooftop chiller (55 E 7th), P1 chiller room and a 2022 BC Hydro primary-service upgrade (555 W Hastings). 515 W Hastings (Harbour Centre East Tower) was added as site 7a: three floors converted to "Bulk Data Storage (F2)" in 2023 ($18 M), sharing the building with SFU's Harbour Centre campus — a campus anchor inside one envelope. 308 E 5th (Atlas Cloud) has no permits; operator pages say a 16-rack room in a leased industrial building, company now inactive on the exchange — kept at 20.

Grid
- Vancouver: the West End Substation CPCN (10 Nov 2025) says downtown substations are "at their final stage of development with no room to add more capacity"; all 12 kV feeder positions in use by fiscal 2027; new 400 MVA substation at 1150 Nelson in service July 2032, $0.76–1.39 B; decision pending. The 2025 IRP follows Bill 31: permanent ban on crypto connections, two-year call limited to 300 MW for AI and 100 MW for data centres; the Call for Demand has 15 applications totalling ~800 MW, outcome deferred. IRP App. F long-run marginal costs: $97/MWh energy, $300/kW·yr capacity, $110/kW·yr distribution — the first public value for flexible load in BC.
- Edmonton: Alta Reg 117/2026 read in full — "large" = ≥75 MW; adjacent parcels aggregated; tethered (to own generation) gets priority; bridged ≤3 years and cut first; all large data centres shed before other customers. AESO October 2026 list: 56 data-load projects, 23,549 MW requested, only 1,752 MW active, none energized; nine projects sized at exactly 74.9 MW. Edmonton planning area: Beacon Saunders Lake 400 MW (under review), Sovereign Cloud 74.9 MW (active, 2029). EPCOR reliability projects name the tight areas (South 51 MW, West/Poundmaker, SW/Meadowlark; Woodcroft N-1 firm 76.0 MVA vs 74.2 peak). Neither city publishes a load-capacity map; ENMAX, OEB and Hydro-Québec do, and the register cites their format.

Heat price window
- Operator floor is not public; the register holds revealed prices (Fortum €11–50/MWh, Stockholm Exergi ~400 SEK/MWh, Denmark cap 95 DKK/GJ) and capex anchors (Wahlroos 2018: 500 €/kW heat, 5-year payback example; Hiltunen & Syri 0.5–0.65 M€/MW).
- Buyer ceiling: Vancouver gas ≈ $38.6/MWh heat, RNG ≈ $70.7, NEU returned-heat credit $32.59; Edmonton gas ≈ $10.7. Edmonton's new City district-energy utility prices on a "business as usual" rule (gas-equivalent plus avoided boiler costs) and its own business case shows decarbonisation is uneconomic at current costs (electric boilers triple operating cost; max-decarbonisation NPV −$172 M) — the gap recovered heat could fill. Tariff (Attachment 4) still to fetch.
- Risk: Lygnerud & Werner 2018 — 6% of Swedish industrial heat recovery lost by volume, but 14 of 19 heat-pump-based recoveries terminated and no large delivery ever lost; a small air-cooled data centre is in the fragile class.

Weather and demand
- CWEC typical-year files computed: full air-side free cooling 7,109 h/yr Vancouver and 7,614 Edmonton at 18 °C inlet, effectively all hours at 27 °C; a dry cooler to a 40 °C loop never needs a chiller in either city. Heating demand is 19:1 winter-to-summer in Vancouver and 9:1 in Edmonton; peak month ÷ minimum month 3.9 and 3.3 — roughly a third of a flat heat output has no taker in summer without storage or a year-round receiver.
- CEUD 2023, provincial: BC households 71.7 GJ/yr, space heat 46% (33 GJ), DHW 27%; Alberta 121.8 GJ, space heat 63% (77 GJ), DHW 23%. Commercial intensity 1.11 GJ/m² BC, 2.01 Alberta. Normals 1991–2020: HDD18 Vancouver Intl 2,781, Edmonton City 5,126 (airport 5,697 — the CWEC file is the airport); rainfall 1,160 mm Vancouver vs 423 mm Edmonton; humidex ≥30 days 5.7 vs 10.5; Edmonton ~6 days/yr with max ≤ −20 °C.

Carbon, groundwater, noise
- Alberta grid displacement factor is a declining schedule (handbook v3.3): 0.49 tCO2e/MWh in 2024 to 0.34 in 2029 (0.52→0.36 with line loss); gas combustion 1,962 g CO2/m³. BC publishes no marginal factor; 22.8 g/kWh average.
- Alberta open-loop ground-source heat exchange = Water Act approval (same aquifer, no loss) or licence (otherwise), with an aquifer test scaled to pumping rate (>265 m³/day → 72+72 h test, observation wells, APEGA report); a 1 MW loop at 5 K ≈ 4,100 m³/day. Closed loops above the base of groundwater protection need no authorization. BC: licence under WSR for open loop. GWELLS export in hand for Vancouver (333 wells in the city); Edmonton wells deferred.
- Noise: AUC Rule 012 Table 1 confirmed, all 15 cells (night 40–56 dBA by density and road proximity, +10 day, receptor within 1.5 km, cumulative). Vancouver By-law 6555 and Edmonton Bylaw 14600 for the building's own plant. Plant sound-power starter set from manufacturer and filed data (Daikin AHRI 370 96–100 dBA; Trane; mtu/Cat ~98 dBA @7 m; Cummins enclosures 73–91).

## 4. Corrections made along the way (so the other chat doesn't repeat them)
- "Half-full data centre = 100% higher cost per watt" is not in Barroso; the text says two-thirds full → +50% opex, half-full server TCO $8,981 vs $6,310.
- The "1,600 MW bridging pool" is an AESO process figure, not in Alta Reg 117/2026.
- Lygnerud & Werner's 6% is volume-weighted over 41 years, not a per-year rate.
- Ayuso-Virgili's thermal share (30%) covers make-up water only.
- West End Substation filing is 10 Nov 2025 (not "Stage 1, 16 Dec 2025").
- CWEC folder naming is bilingual: `CWEC_FMCCE` is CWEC.
- The Vancouver property-tax report has no floor-area field; the 2009 footprints have area and height; BC Assessment's Commercial extract (via UBC Abacus, arranged through Data Librarian Jeremy Buhler) is the floor-area source and is requested.
